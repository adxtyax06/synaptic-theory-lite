"Neuroscience is the study of the nervous system – from structure to function, development to degeneration, in health and in disease." - King's College London, School of Neuroscience<sup>[1]</sup> 

These studies are mainly motivated by advancements in [[Neurology|Medicine & Disease management]]. 

But that is **NOT** what we are here for.

In this resource, we will **NOT** be looking into this from a lens of medicine but from the lens of technology.

The purpose of this course is to deliver a clear understanding of the human brain to Computer Science & AI/ML students and Comp. Sci. concepts to neuroscience students. 

**The real aim** **is two-way development**:

- To **inspire innovation** in AI, ML, Bionics, and related disciplines.

- To **deepen neuroscience itself** by applying insights from these fields.

**Mode of Learning:** We take the **route less taken** — exploring neuroscience through the lenses of **physics, mathematics, and systems theory**, rather than rote medical memorization.

**Relevant Sub-disciplines of Neuroscience:** [[Computational Neuroscience]], [[Neuroinformatics]], [[Bionics & Neuroengineering]]
# Structure of the Course

| Phase | Topic                                                   | Duration | Focus                            |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------- | -------------------------------- |
| 0     | **Foundations (Electricity, Circuits, Biology Basics)** | 3–5 days | Setup physics–biology connection |
| Ia    | **Neuroanatomy**                                        | 2 weeks  | Hardware layout                  |
| Ib    | **Neurophysiology**                                     | 1 month  | Normal signal function           |
| II    | **Neurology (Optional)**                                | —        | Medicine & Pathological insight  |
| III   | **Higher Functions (Thinking, Memory)**                 | 2 weeks  | Computation & cognition          |
| IV    | **Bio vs Artificial Systems**                           | 2 weeks  | Comparison & synthesis           |
