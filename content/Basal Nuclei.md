Basal Ganglia (more aptly, Basal Nuclei since they're a part of the CNS), represent **5 interconnected structures of each side of the brain**. They are subcortical and intracerebral masses of grey matter forming important parts of the extrapyramidal system.
## Components:
1. Caudate Nucleus
2. Putamen
3. Globus Pallidus
4. Subthalamic Nucleus
5. [[Substantia nigra]]
6. Amygdaloid Body?

**Extended Anatomiccal Parts:**
1. Nucleus accumbens
2. Ventral Pallidum
3. Caudolenticular bridges
4. Claustrum?

>Striatum = Caudate + Putamen (dorsal) + Nucleus accumbens (ventral)
>Lentiform nucleus = Putamen + Globus pallidus
>Pallidum = Globus pallidus (GPi + GPe) + Ventral pallidum
>Substantia nigra = SNpc + SNpr

### Functions: 
Input: Motivation from Prefrontal Cortex
Output: Stimulation of Motor Cortex -> Movement
1. Planning and execution of voluntary movements
2. Regulation of subconscious movements
3. Control of muscle tone
4. Control of reflex motor activity
5. Control of automatic associated movements
6. Role in arousal mechanism
## **Internal Circuit**
1. Stimulatory - Cortex, Thalamus (-> Cortex), STN
2. Inhibitory - Striatum (via interneurons), GPi, GPe, SNpr
3. Dual - SNpc

## **Summary**
Motivation for movement arises from prefrontal cortex -> ~={orange}Basal Nuclei Pathway=~ -> Stimulates the motor cortex -> Movement
Without influence of motivation, the direct pathway normally stimulates the thalamus while the indirect pathway inhibits thalamus -> Inhibition = Stimulation, net effect = no effect on motor cortex -> No movement.
~={blue}When motivation for movement arises,=~ SNpc relases Dopamine. Dopamine acts on -
- D1 receptors to stimulate direct pathway -> Stimulates Thalamus -> Stimulates Cortex -> Facillitates movement
- D2 receptors to inhibit indirect pathway -> Decreased Inhibition of Thalamus = Stimulation of Thalamus -> Facillitates movement
